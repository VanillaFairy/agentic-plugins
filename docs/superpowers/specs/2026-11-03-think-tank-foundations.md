# think-tank foundations

These are the rules of thinking the plugin is built on. They describe the topology of the thinking
matter: what exists in a discussion, how it relates, and how the discussion moves. They say nothing
about how any of it is implemented. Each rule says something no other rule says.

Every mechanism in the plugin exists because one of these rules demands it, and names the rule it
serves. A mechanism that serves no rule is removed. The rules change only by the user's decision.

## Statements

**1. Questions are judged by relevance, never refuted.** A question widens the space. It's admitted
when it traces back to the purpose; one that doesn't is set aside and kept, not argued.
*Rules out:* arguing with a question, and digressions that live on because they're interesting.

**2. Every statement carries where it came from.** It's observed (checkable outside the
discussion), inferred, or assumed. A statement is never stronger than the weakest thing it rests
on, and agreement among participants never turns it into an observation.
*Rules out:* confidence laundered through repetition.

**3. Meaning comes before argument.** When a disagreement rests on a word, the word's meaning is
settled first.
*Rules out:* arguing past each other.

## Participants

**4. Every perspective is a bias, opposed from at least two directions.** No perspective goes
unopposed, and its opponents don't all pull the same way.
*Rules out:* a lone advocate, and opposition that is one viewpoint in several voices.

**5. The work summons its participants.** Whenever the work needs a participant no one present can
be, such as a facet no one can see, one is called up, at the start or at any point after. A
participant with nothing left to do is released.
*Rules out:* a fixed cast decided up front.

**6. No one holds the whole.** Each participant attends to a bounded part of the space, what it's
asked to address and what that rests on, and reaches further only by following connections.
*Rules out:* every participant reading everything, and any participant that must understand the
whole to act.

**7. No one argues and judges the same matter.** Arguing, judging and organizing are different kinds
of work, held by different participants.
*Rules out:* a participant grading its own position, and one orchestrator carrying every decision.

## Knowledge

**8. The present links to its past.** The current understanding of each topic links to everything
it superseded, rejected or improved on, and why.
*Rules out:* a summary that hides how it was reached.

**9. Nothing is rewritten.** Every change to the shared understanding is recorded with what changed,
who changed it and why, and the record is never edited.
*Rules out:* silent changes, and a conclusion whose origin can't be traced.

## Settling

**10. Agreement is earned.** A proposal stands only after it has survived a real attempt to refute
it; agreement nobody contested is a hypothesis. When rival proposals both survive, the question
stays open with the crux between them named, along with what would decide it.
*Rules out:* consensus by politeness, and a stalemate nobody can act on.

**11. The human is sovereign over purpose and values.** The human may speak at any moment, and their
word reshapes the space: it can redirect, settle, reopen or set aside anything. It's built on, never
argued with. Participants ask the human only what is theirs to decide.
*Rules out:* participants deciding taste or priority, and a human consulted only at the end.

## The procedure

**12. The procedure is thinking matter too.** The way the plugin thinks changes only through a run
of this same process that the human starts, working from the evidence of real runs.
*Rules out:* a procedure that changes itself, and changes argued from imagined runs.
